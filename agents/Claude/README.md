## What each file is for

**`Summary of Only Necessary Context.md`** — The handoff to my own next session. Completely rewritten at the end of every session, because a new session starts with no memory of this one. It carries the current state, the decisions made and why, the open questions, and the next steps. It deliberately does **not** repeat anything in `Project Details/` or `AgentPrompt.md`, since those are re-read in full at the start of every session.

**`Literature Foundation.md`** — The Phase 0 survey of the spike-sorting validation field: methods landscape, benchmark ranges, datasets and licenses, failure modes, open questions, references. Its job is to make sure the Claim Sheet's method choices and success bars are informed by what the field has established rather than invented on the spot. **It is a dated artifact and is frozen as written** — corrections to it propagate forward into `references.md` and into later work, not backward into the document.

**`references.md`** — The running ledger of every source that informed my work: what it covers, how it shaped a decision, a verified link, and a citation ready to transfer into the Technical Report's bibliography. **This is the living document.** Where it and the Literature Foundation disagree, this file governs. It also carries a *Pending* section for sources located but not yet verified, which are not citable until they move up, and a **corrections log** at the top recording every claim later evidence overturned — kept visible rather than deleted, so the trail stays auditable. Entries are tagged `[ANCHOR]`, `[VERIFY]` (unconfirmed claim), `[CLEARED]` (formerly unconfirmed, now verified, with who verified it), and `[SUPERSEDED]` (wrong, with the correction alongside).

**`Session Summaries/HumanReport<N>.md`** — What happened in session N, written for the director: what was done, what was decided and why, what was hard, what files moved, and what comes next. Sequential and append-only as a set; earlier reports are never edited.

**`tools/`** — Scripts that support review but do not belong inside the packet, because they reproduce no recorded result and a reader copying the packet out should not be told to run them. Seven so far, all of them tests or probes: the one command that briefly lived here, `measure_host_drift.py`, moved into the packet's `scripts/` folder in Session 28 when review found that a command a reader cannot run from its documented location is not a reproducible command. `mutation_test_runbook_checker.py` breaks a throwaway copy of the packet eighteen different ways and confirms `check_runbook_consistency.py` catches each one, plus an unmutated control -- **Session 29 added the last three**, which are the ones that hold the `PENDING_STEP` exemption to account: remove the declaration and the stepless script must be reported, declare a script that already has a step and that must be an error, and delete a declared script and the stale declaration must be caught; it takes the packet path, a scratch directory and the interpreter as arguments and never touches the real packet. `zone_provenance_headroom.py` reads the pinned donor snapshot and reports, for each provenance level, how many injection-zone donors a source group holds against how many non-zone templates sit in that same group — the ceiling on what the matching rule's blocking stages can work with, before any host exists. Its supply figures are an upper bound and it says so; host-specific eligibility can only cut them. `source_count_granularity_probe.py` measures what a distinct-`dataset` count actually constrains: it asserts that the column is the probe-insertion identifier and that session and subject are determined by it, then censuses how many source subsets of each size span that many animals. **This is the one tool whose output another artifact cites** — Draft 4 of the matching rule quotes its numbers — so its report is recorded beside it at `source_count_granularity_probe_2026-08-13.txt` rather than left to be re-run. Offline, stdlib, pinned snapshot only. `test_band_drift.py` is the synthetic test battery for `Reproducibility Packet/scripts/utils/band_drift.py` — 103 checks over inputs constructed so the answer is known before the test: a ramp of a stated size, a trajectory that returns to where it started, a trace whose worst window is deliberately not its first, a band left one unit short in a single bin, a band where a minority of units moves and the across-unit median does not follow, and the determinism of the pinned permutation seeds. Six of its cases exist because review caught the corresponding defects in the specification, so they are the ones worth keeping — the fourth is Codex's Session 21 counterexample, kept as a permanent case rather than left in a review probe, the fifth holds the two Session 23 fixtures that show why the per-unit audit values cannot be read against the band's own null and why the scatter of their window starts is not evidence either, and the sixth holds Session 24's masking fixture — ten of twenty-one units genuinely moving 30 µm while the candidate passes both gate numbers and no magnitude separation is visible — beside the check that Codex's ordering counterexample does not need its exactly-flat unit. **Session 25 added the gate-window case** — the two constructions that showed a ten-bin window does not span ten minutes — and **Session 26 added the within-bin transmission case**, which holds Codex's RC-001 Round-2 counterexample against my own overgeneralized cutoff: a spread within-bin depth distribution passes a 30 µm episode through as `29.000 µm` at a displaced fraction of one fiftieth, a single displaced spike in a hundred moves that bin's median `14.500 µm`, the same displaced spikes read `30.000 µm` inside one bin and `0.000 µm` split across two, and the rank-and-offset bound that covers every case between the two fixtures holds at zero violations over 4,000 random cases across four depth families. It defaults to the gate's own 200 permutations rather than a faster number, so what is tested is what will run. It reads no archive data and needs no network. `probe_band_drift_claims.py` is the Session 19 owner-review probe for the same module, and it checks claims rather than behaviour: it restores the pre-repair full-array permutation on a copy of the module and reproduces the partial-bin contamination defect that repair removed, confirms on 200 randomized fixtures that the permuted pool is exactly the complete-bin index set, and confirms the bound §16.5 asserts — that no value of the null lets a candidate whose *observed* excursion exceeds the tolerance pass. It takes the module path as an argument and reads no archive data. **Session 27 added the last two.** `measure_host_drift.py` is the command that turns one candidate host into a drift verdict: it derives the band from the raw file's electrode table, reads only that band's units out of the processed file through the new packet module `utils/archive_units.py`, confirms the four input properties Section 16.8 requires, calls the approved estimator, replays the permutation null to prove it reproduces, and applies the gate at a threshold that cannot be typed on the command line -- `--gate` resolves to the pre-declared strict or relaxed value and there is no third option. An input error stops it without a verdict, deliberately, because the host order is first-admissible and a candidate recorded as failing for a reason that is not drift is handed to the next rank irrecoverably. `test_measure_host_drift.py` runs that command end to end against local HDF5 fixtures shaped like the two assets, with at least one deliberately broken fixture for every confirmation, and with the per-unit audit values compared elementwise against a direct estimator call rather than recomputed. Neither reads the archive. **Session 28 took it from 163 checks to 231, and Session 29 to 266** in response to Codex's RC-002 Round 1, and added a block-caching fixture reader so that what a read *costs* can be tested rather than assumed: the transfer bound is asserted to cover the bytes a block-fetching reader actually moves. It also stopped a raised exception from ending the whole run, which had been letting a total describe a smaller suite than the one that was asked for. **Session 28 added the eighth tool, and it is the one worth reading first.** `mutate_rc002_repairs.py` exists because a suite written after a repair can encode the repair rather than the property: it removes one repair in its own clean copy of the tree, runs the suite there, and requires the named cases to go red. **Session 29 took it from eight mutations to thirteen** for the Round-2 repairs, all caught, with a green unmutated control -- and corrected its own coverage claim in the same pass. It does **not** cover Codex's F5, and its docstring now says why rather than counting it: F5's repair was moving the command into the packet and declaring it pending in the runbook checker, and neither is a string a text-mutation harness can revert. The command's own `sys.path` line looks like the candidate and is not one, because CPython puts a directly executed script's directory on `sys.path` anyway. The two halves are covered instead by a new subprocess `--help` case in the acceptance suite and by the three new checker mutations above. It takes `--repo-root` and writes nothing outside a temporary directory.

## How to navigate this folder with no prior context

1. **Read `Summary of Only Necessary Context.md`.** It is written for exactly this situation and will orient you faster than anything else here.
2. **Read the most recent `Session Summaries/HumanReport<N>.md`** for the narrative of how the work got where it is.
3. **Open `Literature Foundation.md`** only when you need the field grounding — method choices, realistic benchmark ranges, known dead ends, or the reasoning behind an axis choice. It is long by design; §5 (open questions) is the part that feeds the Claim Sheet most directly.
4. **Use `references.md` as the citation source of truth.** Do not cite anything from memory, and do not cite anything sitting in its *Pending* section.

If you are Codex: the chats folder is the right place to raise anything about this work, not an edit to these files. The exception is the review cycle — when an artifact I own is formally handed to you for review, you may edit it directly and hand it back stating what changed, per `Playbooks/review-cycle.md`.
