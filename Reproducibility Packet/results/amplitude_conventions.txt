3. The conversion, measured on the same units
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  For every unit in this file: nan-aware peak-to-peak of `waveform_mean`
  over time, on the channel maximising it, converted volts -> microvolts.
  That is the donor column's definition evaluated on host units. Each
  unit's value is then divided by its own `median_spike_amplitude_uV`.

  A third convention difference showed up here and is reported rather than
  smoothed over. The file's `max_electrode` is chosen by IBL's own rule,
  not by peak-to-peak of this mean waveform, and the two agree on only
  72.6% of units -- usually a near tie between adjacent
  contacts. Every ratio below is therefore given twice: on the channel the
  upstream rule picks, and on the electrode the file itself names. They
  agree closely, so the conversion does not rest on the channel choice.

5. What this establishes, and what it does not
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Establishes: the two columns are NOT the same quantity, and the
  conversion between their definitions is measured above on real units.
  Any comparison of the 50-200 uV donor target against host amplitudes
  must apply that conversion first.

  Does not establish: that the converted numbers are directly comparable.
  The donor averages are built on a 1 Hz highpass plus common median
  reference over the last 30 minutes of good clusters only, while the host
  column is IBL's own number on IBL's own preprocessing over the whole
  recording. Preprocessing is a second difference this report does not
  measure and does not claim to have removed.

  One session. It fixes the convention, not the population.
